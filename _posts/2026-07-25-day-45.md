what_i_learned: |
  Today began the SAIRI Symposium, bringing together students, researchers, faculty members, mentors, and guests to celebrate the work completed throughout the summer. The opening ceremony created an exciting atmosphere and gave us a clear understanding of what to expect during the event.

  The keynote speaker discussed the value of curiosity, teamwork, and using research to solve meaningful problems. Listening to the presentation reminded me that research is not only about collecting data or producing results. It also requires asking important questions, learning from others, and finding ways to turn ideas into solutions that can benefit real communities.

  One of the most meaningful moments of the day was attending my graduate mentor's poster presentation about DermaBridge. Watching the project being explained to visitors gave me a new perspective on the research we had developed as a team. I observed how my mentor introduced the problem, described our methods, presented our findings, and responded to questions from people with different levels of technical knowledge.

  This experience helped me understand how important research communication is. A strong project must not only be technically sound but also presented in a way that allows others to recognize its purpose, impact, and future potential.

blockers: |
  A challenge during the symposium was understanding how to summarize a detailed research project for people who may not be familiar with artificial intelligence, machine learning, medical imaging, or dermatology.

  DermaBridge includes several important areas, such as dataset preparation, model development, fairness across skin tones, explainable risk assessment, mobile application design, and clinical referral support. Presenting all of these ideas without overwhelming the audience requires careful organization and clear language.

  I also recognized that presenters must be prepared for unexpected questions. Some attendees may focus on model accuracy, while others may be more interested in healthcare access, privacy, fairness, or how the mobile application could be used in real-life situations. Observing these conversations helped me think about how I could prepare for our own presentation.

reflection: |
  The first day of the symposium allowed me to experience our summer research from the perspective of an audience member. Throughout the program, I had been deeply involved in the development process, but seeing people encounter DermaBridge for the first time helped me appreciate how far the project had progressed.

  It was encouraging to see attendees pause at the poster, study the information, and ask thoughtful questions. Their interest showed that the challenges DermaBridge addresses are important and that our work has the potential to contribute to broader discussions about healthcare technology, early skin-cancer screening, responsible artificial intelligence, and fairness for people with darker skin tones.

  I was especially proud to support my graduate mentor during the presentation. Their guidance played an important role in our team's progress throughout the summer, and watching them confidently represent the project was both motivating and educational.

  Today also gave me valuable ideas for improving my own presentation skills. I paid attention to how information was organized, how technical concepts were simplified, and how questions were answered clearly and honestly. I plan to use what I observed as our team prepares to present DermaBridge during the next part of the symposium.

  Overall, the first day was an inspiring introduction to the event. It reminded me that research becomes more powerful when it is shared, discussed, questioned, and connected to the needs of others. I am looking forward to presenting our work, learning from the other researchers, and continuing to grow from this experience.
