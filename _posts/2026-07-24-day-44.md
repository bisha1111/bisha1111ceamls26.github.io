what_i_learned: |
  Today began in the lab, where our team continued making the final adjustments to our DermaBridge presentation. We reviewed our speaking sections, checked the organization of the slides, and discussed how we could explain our research clearly to people with different levels of technical knowledge. This final preparation gave us another opportunity to correct small issues and ensure that each part of the presentation connected smoothly.

  Later, we visited the venue where we would present our research during the SAIRI Symposium. Walking through the space allowed us to see where the posters would be displayed, where the presentations would take place, and how attendees would move around the room. Seeing the location in advance made the symposium feel more real, but it also helped reduce some of the nervousness surrounding the presentation.

  Being able to picture ourselves presenting in the space helped us think more carefully about our delivery, positioning, volume, and interaction with the audience. Instead of entering an unfamiliar environment on the day of the symposium, we now had a better idea of what to expect and could prepare ourselves mentally.

  After the venue tour, we participated in the SAIRI Field Day. Everyone wore the tie-dye shirts we had created earlier in the week, which gave the event a colorful and energetic atmosphere. We took part in several group activities, including Capture the Flag and the Blind Relay, along with other team-based games.

  These activities required us to communicate clearly, listen to one another, make quick decisions, and trust our teammates. Although the games were competitive, they also provided a fun opportunity to connect with students from the other research groups outside of the usual laboratory setting.

blockers: |
  One challenge today was balancing the final presentation work with the other activities scheduled throughout the day. We still had important details to review, but we also needed to participate fully in the venue tour and Field Day.

  During our presentation review, we noticed that some sections still contained too much information. We had to shorten certain explanations while making sure that the most important points about DermaBridge remained clear. We also worked on improving the transitions between speakers so the presentation would feel unified instead of sounding like several separate parts.

  The Field Day activities presented a different kind of challenge. Games such as the Blind Relay required participants to depend completely on the instructions of their teammates. Miscommunication could quickly cause confusion, so everyone had to speak clearly, remain patient, and pay close attention.

reflection: |
  Today provided a meaningful balance between serious preparation and team bonding. Spending time in the lab reminded us that the symposium was approaching quickly and that every final detail mattered. At the same time, visiting the venue helped transform the event from something we had only discussed into something we could clearly imagine.

  I felt more comfortable about the upcoming presentation after seeing the space. Knowing where we would stand, where our poster would be placed, and how the room was arranged gave me a greater sense of confidence and readiness.

  Field Day was also a memorable part of the experience. Throughout the summer, most of our teamwork involved research, coding, data analysis, writing, and application development. Today allowed us to work together in a completely different environment.

  The activities showed that teamwork is not only important when solving technical problems. It also depends on trust, patience, communication, encouragement, and the willingness to support one another. These are the same qualities that helped our team make progress on DermaBridge throughout the program.

  Wearing our tie-dye shirts and participating in the activities together created a strong sense of community. It was a reminder that SAIRI was not only about completing a research project. It was also about building relationships, learning how to collaborate, and creating memories with people who shared similar goals.

  By the end of the day, I felt both excited and prepared for the symposium. Our presentation was nearly complete, we had become familiar with the venue, and our team had strengthened its connection through the Field Day activities. Today helped us step away from the pressure of research for a moment while still developing the communication and teamwork skills that would support us during our final presentation.
