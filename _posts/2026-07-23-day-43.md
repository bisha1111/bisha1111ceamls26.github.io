what_i_learned: |
  Today, our team completed a full rehearsal of the DermaBridge presentation in front of our program coordinator. This was an important step in preparing for the SAIRI Research Symposium because it allowed us to experience what it would feel like to explain our work to someone outside our immediate research group.

  During the rehearsal, we walked through the entire presentation, explained the motivation behind DermaBridge, discussed our research process, and shared the progress we made on the machine learning models and mobile application. Presenting everything from beginning to end helped us notice areas that still needed to be refined before the symposium.

  Our coordinator offered detailed suggestions about the structure and appearance of several slides. Some information needed to be rearranged, while other sections needed to be simplified so that the audience could follow the presentation more easily. This taught me that a strong slide should support the speaker rather than compete with everything the speaker is trying to say.

  I also learned more about the difference between memorizing information and genuinely understanding it. We were encouraged to speak in a conversational way, maintain eye contact, and explain the project using our own words instead of depending heavily on the text displayed on the screen. This advice helped me realize that confidence comes from knowing the research well enough to communicate it naturally.

blockers: |
  One difficulty was making sure that every team member could explain their section clearly without sounding rehearsed or disconnected from the rest of the presentation. Because each person worked on different areas of the project, we needed to make the transitions feel smooth and ensure that the presentation sounded like one complete story.

  We also had to determine how much technical detail to include. DermaBridge involves medical image datasets, machine learning models, fairness evaluation, explainability, application development, and clinical referral support. Including too much detail could confuse the audience, but removing too much could make the project appear less substantial.

  Another challenge was controlling nervous habits such as speaking too quickly, looking at the slides too often, or forgetting to pause between important points. The rehearsal made these habits easier to recognize, giving us time to correct them before the final presentation.

reflection: |
  The practice session was valuable because it gave us an honest picture of our current level of preparation. Instead of waiting until the symposium to discover weaknesses, we were able to identify them early and make focused improvements.

  The feedback was constructive, but it was also very encouraging. Our coordinator praised the visual quality of the slides, the organization of our research, and the importance of the problem DermaBridge is designed to address. Hearing those comments reassured us that the effort we invested throughout the summer was visible in the final presentation.

  I felt especially proud that our project was recognized for combining technical research with a meaningful healthcare purpose. DermaBridge is not only about training an artificial intelligence model. It is also about improving access to early skin-cancer screening, promoting fairness across different skin tones, and helping users better understand when they may need professional care.

  Today reminded me that presenting research is a skill of its own. Completing strong technical work is important, but the impact of that work can be lost if it is not communicated clearly. A presenter must understand the audience, explain complex ideas simply, and create a connection between the research and the real-world problem it addresses.

  By the end of the rehearsal, I felt more aware of what I needed to improve and more confident about the progress our team had made. We left with specific changes to complete, stronger ideas for our delivery, and greater motivation to give a presentation that represents the full value of the DermaBridge project.
