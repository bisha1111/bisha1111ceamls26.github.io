what_i_learned: |
  Today, our team concentrated on bringing the DermaBridge presentation closer to its final form. Rather than simply adding more content, we examined the presentation from the audience's perspective and asked whether each slide clearly contributed to the story we wanted to tell.

  We reorganized several sections so that the presentation moved logically from the healthcare problem to our proposed solution, research methods, model development, fairness considerations, mobile application, and future plans. This process showed me that a research presentation should feel like one connected narrative instead of a collection of unrelated facts.

  We also improved the visual appearance of the deck. We checked the spacing, text sizes, headings, image placement, and overall structure of every slide. Keeping the same visual style throughout the presentation made the work appear more polished and helped prevent unnecessary distractions.

  After completing the major revisions, we rehearsed the presentation as a full team. Each person delivered the portion connected to their work while the rest of the group listened and took notes. This allowed us to hear how the individual sections sounded when combined into one complete presentation.

blockers: |
  One challenge was deciding what information to remove. Our team completed a large amount of work during the program, and we naturally wanted to explain every part of it. However, including too many details would make the presentation difficult to follow and could cause us to exceed the time limit.

  We also noticed that some slides repeated ideas that were already explained elsewhere. We had to combine similar points, shorten long descriptions, and replace some paragraphs with visuals or brief statements.

  During the rehearsal, a few transitions between speakers felt sudden. In some places, one section ended without clearly introducing the next topic. We worked on connecting those sections so that the audience would understand how the different parts of DermaBridge related to one another.

  Timing was another area that required attention. Some team members moved quickly through their sections, while others needed more time to explain technical information. We had to adjust our pacing so that everyone could speak clearly without making the overall presentation too long.

reflection: |
  Today helped me understand that finalizing a presentation involves much more than correcting grammar or choosing attractive slides. Every design choice, explanation, and transition affects how the audience understands the research.

  Rehearsing together gave us the chance to notice problems that were not obvious while editing the slides individually. Hearing the full presentation revealed where the story became unclear, where explanations were too technical, and where we needed stronger connections between ideas.

  I also became more aware of the importance of speaking naturally. Reading every sentence from a slide can make a presentation feel distant, even when the research is meaningful. The practice session encouraged me to focus on understanding my section well enough to explain it confidently in my own words.

  Working as a group was especially valuable because everyone contributed a different perspective. A slide that seemed clear to one person sometimes confused another, and those discussions helped us improve the final result. We listened to one another's suggestions and made decisions based on what would make the presentation strongest as a whole.

  By the end of the day, our presentation felt more organized, visually consistent, and connected. We still had a few details to improve, but the rehearsal showed that we were making strong progress toward presenting DermaBridge in a clear, professional, and memorable way.
