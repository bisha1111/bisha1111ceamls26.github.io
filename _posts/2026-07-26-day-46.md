what_i_learned: |
  The final day of the SAIRI Symposium brought our presentation experience to a close in a way I will always remember. After weeks of researching, testing, writing, designing, and preparing, our team finally had the opportunity to introduce DermaBridge to a wider audience.

  We presented our work to researchers, faculty members, students, and other guests who were interested in learning about the purpose of the project. We explained how we prepared our datasets, developed and evaluated our machine learning models, examined fairness concerns, and designed a mobile application to support accessible skin-cancer screening and clinical referrals.

  Speaking with attendees showed me how important it is to communicate technical ideas in a way that people from different backgrounds can understand. Their questions encouraged us to think more deeply about our methods, the limitations of our research, and the possibilities for expanding DermaBridge in the future.

blockers: |
  One challenge was presenting several weeks of detailed research within a limited amount of time. Our project included data preparation, machine learning, fairness evaluation, mobile application development, research writing, and healthcare considerations, so we had to carefully decide which information would be most valuable to our audience.

  We also needed to prepare for questions about model performance, the representation of different skin tones, limitations within the datasets, and the current stage of the mobile application. Responding to these questions required us to clearly explain both the progress we had made and the areas that would require further research and development.

reflection: |
  Watching people stop at our poster, listen to our presentation, and ask thoughtful questions gave new meaning to everything we had worked on throughout the summer. It reminded me that DermaBridge was not simply an academic assignment. It was a project capable of creating important conversations about healthcare access, responsible artificial intelligence, fairness, and the communities technology is intended to serve.

  The day became even more memorable when our team received two special recognitions. Our DermaBridge pitch video earned third place in the Project Spotlight Video Competition, and our team won the award for Best Research Poster.

  These awards represented more than the final results. They reflected the long meetings, technical challenges, repeated revisions, creative decisions, and teamwork that shaped the project. Every member of the team contributed something meaningful, and receiving these honors together made the moment even more rewarding.

  Finishing the symposium with these achievements gave us a proud memory to carry from the SAIRI experience. I am grateful for the opportunity to present research that combines technology with an important healthcare purpose. The experience strengthened my confidence as a researcher, developer, presenter, and collaborator while encouraging me to continue creating solutions that are ethical, inclusive, and capable of improving people's lives.
