what_i_learned: |
  Today was the official conclusion of the SAIRI Summer Research Program. Reaching the end of these nine weeks gave me an opportunity to pause and recognize how much this experience has shaped me as a student, researcher, developer, and team member.

  When the program began, many parts of the research process were still unfamiliar to me. Throughout the summer, I gained practical experience working with artificial intelligence, machine learning, medical image datasets, data preprocessing, model evaluation, fairness analysis, and responsible AI. I became more comfortable preparing large datasets, comparing different machine learning approaches, interpreting performance metrics, identifying possible sources of bias, and thinking carefully about how an AI system may affect people from different backgrounds.

  Through the DermaBridge project, I learned that developing a healthcare technology solution requires more than creating an accurate model. A responsible system must also be understandable, accessible, fair, and useful to the people it is designed to serve. Our work encouraged me to think beyond technical performance and consider explainability, patient trust, skin-tone representation, privacy, clinical referrals, and the real-world limitations of AI-assisted screening.

  I also expanded my software development skills by contributing to the creation of the DermaBridge mobile application. Working with Android Studio allowed me to explore mobile interface design, application navigation, camera functionality, accessibility features, patient-information workflows, and the process of connecting an AI model to a usable application. There were moments when the application did not behave as expected, but resolving those issues taught me patience, persistence, and the importance of testing each component carefully.

  Another major part of the program was learning how to communicate technical research effectively. I participated in writing sections of our research paper, organizing our findings, developing presentation slides, preparing our project pitch, and explaining complex ideas in language that a broader audience could understand.

  Presenting our work at the National Symposium on Effective and Ethical AI was one of the most meaningful moments of the summer. It gave me the opportunity to share our progress, answer questions, and see how our project connected to larger conversations about responsible technology.

blockers: |
  The journey was not without difficulties. Our team had to work through incomplete metadata, uneven representation across diagnostic categories and skin tones, model limitations, software errors, time constraints, and the challenge of deciding how much information to include in our final research materials.

  Some technical problems required us to revisit earlier decisions, adjust our methods, and test new approaches. At times, it was difficult to balance model development, application design, research writing, presentation preparation, and fairness evaluation within the program schedule.

  We also had to be honest about what our prototype could currently accomplish and what would require additional development beyond the summer. These obstacles became an important part of the learning process.

  Instead of viewing every issue as a failure, I learned to treat it as evidence that something needed to be investigated more carefully. Asking questions, reviewing documentation, seeking feedback from our mentors, and working closely with my teammates helped us continue moving forward.

reflection: |
  As I reflect on the entire SAIRI experience, I feel proud of the progress our team made and grateful for every person who contributed to the journey. I entered the program eager to strengthen my knowledge of artificial intelligence, but I am leaving with much more than technical experience.

  I developed greater confidence in my ability to conduct research, solve unfamiliar problems, communicate my ideas, receive constructive criticism, and contribute meaningfully to a team.

  Working on DermaBridge was especially personal and meaningful because the project addressed a serious healthcare challenge. The possibility of using technology to support earlier skin-cancer screening, improve access to understandable health information, and reduce disparities for people with darker skin tones reminded me why responsible innovation matters.

  Technology should not only be impressive. It should improve lives, protect users, and include communities that are often overlooked.

  I am thankful to my faculty mentor, graduate mentor, teammates, program organizers, and everyone who offered guidance throughout the summer. Their support challenged me to think more deeply, improve the quality of my work, and recognize areas where I still have room to grow.

  The collaboration within our team showed me that strong research is rarely completed alone. It requires people with different strengths who are willing to listen, contribute, adapt, and remain committed to a shared purpose.

  Although today is the final day of the SAIRI Summer Research Program, I do not see it as the end of my research journey. The knowledge, relationships, and confidence I gained will continue to influence my education and future career.

  I plan to build upon the work we started, continue exploring trustworthy artificial intelligence, strengthen my machine learning and mobile development skills, and pursue opportunities where technology can be used to address meaningful social and healthcare problems.

  These nine weeks challenged me, inspired me, and showed me that I am capable of accomplishing more than I initially imagined. I am leaving the program with gratitude for how far I have come, excitement about what lies ahead, and a stronger commitment to creating technology that is ethical, inclusive, and centered on the people it is meant to serve.
